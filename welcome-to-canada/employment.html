Writing a Canadian Resume

Key Differences from Other Countries:

  • NO photo - Don't include your photo
  • NO date of birth - Not needed or wanted
  • NO marital status - Keep personal life private
  • Language skills IMPORTANT - State English/French fluency
  • Canadian experience valued - Highlight any Canadian work
Resume Structure (1-2 pages max):
1
Contact Information

Full name, phone, email, city/province, LinkedIn (optional)

2
Professional Summary

3-4 lines about your skills and goals (optional but helpful)

3
Work Experience

Most recent job first. Include job title, company, dates, achievements

4
Education

Degree, school, graduation date. List Canadian credentials if you have any.

5
Skills

Technical skills, languages, software (Excel, Salesforce, etc.)

Free Resume Help: Settlement agencies often offer FREE resume workshops for newcomers!

Professional Credential Recognition

Important for Regulated Professions:

Some professions need Canadian certification (doctors, nurses, engineers, accountants, etc.)

  • Contact your provincial regulatory body
  • Many require exams or additional training
  • Timeline can be 6-18 months
  • Cost varies ($1,000-$5,000+)

Understanding Your Pay Stub

Your pay stub (paycheck) shows:
Gross Pay

Total salary before deductions

Example: $2,500

Federal Income Tax

Taxes for federal government

Example: -$250 (depends on salary)

Provincial Income Tax

Taxes for provincial government

Example: -$150

CPP (Canada Pension Plan)

Retirement savings (automatic)

Example: -$150

EI (Employment Insurance)

Insurance if you lose your job

Example: -$60

Net Pay

Amount you actually receive

Example: $1,890

Know Your Employment Rights

As a PR, you have the SAME employment rights as Canadian citizens!
Minimum Wage

Federal: $16.65/hour (2024)

Each province can set higher

Vacation Days

Minimum 2 weeks per year (varies by province)

Overtime Pay

Must be paid for hours over 40/week

Usually 1.5x regular pay

Safe Workplace

Employer must provide safe working conditions

Tips for Canadian Job Interviews

  • Be on time (or slightly early) - 15 minutes before
  • Firm handshake - Professional and confident
  • Dress professionally - Business casual or formal dress
  • Make eye contact - Shows confidence and respect
  • Use examples - "Tell me about a time when..." responses
  • Ask questions - Shows you're interested
  • Follow up - Send thank you email after interview
