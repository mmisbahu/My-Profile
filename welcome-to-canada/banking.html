Banking in Canada

How to open a bank account and understand banking in Canada

Why You Need a Bank Account

Direct Deposit

Employers deposit your salary directly to your account

Debit Card

Pay bills, buy groceries, withdraw cash from ATMs

Credit History

Build Canadian credit for loans, mortgages, apartment rentals

Safety

Secure place to store your money (insured by CDIC)

When to Open Your Account

Open within your first week! Most banks have newcomer packages and staff who understand your situation.

Steps to Open an Account

1
Find a Bank & Visit

Major banks have branches everywhere. Go to any branch.

2
Show Your Documents

Bring: Passport, COPR, proof of Canadian address (lease, utility bill, or letter from friend)

3
Choose Account Type

Most offer "Newcomer Packages" with no minimum balance and free banking for 6-12 months

4
Deposit Initial Amount

Deposit $100+ (varies by bank). Can be cash or check.

5
Get Your Cards

Receive debit card (immediately or in 5 days) and start using your account

Documents You'll Need

Required:
  • Valid Passport
  • COPR (Confirmation of Permanent Residence)
  • Proof of Canadian Address:
    • Lease/rental agreement
    • Utility bill (gas, hydro, internet)
    • Letter from landlord or friend
    • Hotel bill with address

Building Credit & Credit Cards

Why Build Credit?

  • Get better interest rates on loans
  • Rent apartments more easily
  • Qualify for mortgages
  • Get credit cards with rewards

How to Build Credit:

  • Use your debit card frequently
  • Apply for a "Secured Credit Card"
  • Pay all bills on time
  • Keep credit card balance low
No Credit History? Most newcomers don't have a Canadian credit score. Start with a secured credit card, which requires a deposit. After 6-12 months of good payment history, you can get a regular card.
