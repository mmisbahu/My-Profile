Healthcare in Canada

How Canadian healthcare works and how to access medical services

How Canadian Healthcare Works

The Canadian System is Public & FREE!

Canada has universal healthcare - all permanent residents and citizens have access to free basic medical services.

  • Doctor visits: FREE (covered by provincial plan)
  • Hospital stays: FREE
  • Ambulance: FREE (in emergencies)
  • Prescriptions: NOT free (you pay at pharmacy, though some have insurance)
  • Dental & Vision: NOT covered (you pay out of pocket or private insurance)
Emergency Care: Can access emergency room immediately, even without provincial health card. Just bring ID.

Finding a Family Doctor

Steps:
1
Call or visit doctor's office

Ask if they're taking new patients

2
Fill out patient forms

Medical history, medications, allergies

3
Book appointment

Usually 2-4 weeks wait for first appointment

Ways to Find a Doctor:
  • Your health unit website - Most provinces have doctor finders
  • Ask your settlement agency - They have lists of welcoming doctors
  • Walk-in clinics - No appointment needed (wait time varies)
  • Virtual doctors - Many provinces offer telehealth options
Long Waits: it can take months to find a family doctor. In the meantime, use walk-in clinics or urgent care centers.

When You Need Quick Help

Walk-In Clinic

No appointment, see doctor in 1-4 hours

Cost: FREE (with health card)

Good for: Minor injuries, common illnesses

Telehealth

Phone or video call with doctor

Cost: FREE or small fee

Good for: Quick advice, non-emergency

Urgent Care Centers

Open 24/7, trained for emergencies

Cost: FREE (for emergencies)

Good for: Serious but non-life-threatening

Emergency Room (Hospital)

For life-threatening emergencies

Cost: FREE

Call: 911 for ambulance

Prescriptions & Medications

How to Get Prescription Medications:
  1. Doctor writes prescription after your visit
  2. Take prescription to any pharmacy (Shoppers Drug Mart, Rexall, Costco Pharmacy, etc.)
  3. Pay for the medication (costs vary)
  4. Pick up usually within 24 hours

Ways to Save on Medications:

  • Ask for generic versions (much cheaper than brand names)
  • Check if your employer offers drug benefits
  • Compare prices at different pharmacies
  • Look for coupons or pharmacist assistance programs

Mental Health Support

Mental health is important! Many newcomers experience stress, anxiety, or homesickness. Support is free and available.
Counseling Services

Usually FREE through settlement agencies or community health centers

Crisis Helplines

Canada Suicide Prevention Service: 1-833-456-4566

Dental & Vision (NOT Covered by Healthcare)

Dental

NOT free except for children (some provinces)

Cost: Cleaning $100-200, fillings $200-500

Options: Private dental insurance, community health centers

Vision

NOT free except for children (some provinces)

Cost: Eye exam $100-200, glasses/contacts $200-600

Options: Private insurance, budget optometrists

Bringing Medications to Canada

You can bring prescription medications you need personally to Canada, but they must be:
  • In original labeled bottles with your name
  • For personal use only (not to sell)
  • Declare them at customs
